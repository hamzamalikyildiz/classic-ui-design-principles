Recipe · Late winter

Blood orange and fennel, sliced thin enough to see through

A salad that asks for almost nothing: good oil, a sharp knife and the patience to wait out the last cold weeks before the markets fill with green.

By Mara Okafor

Cooking editor · 9 min read · Serves 4

Every February I forget that winter is a fruit season. The oranges arrive in crates with their skins the colour of dried clay, and by the time you cut one open the inside has turned the deep red of a bruise that someone decided to celebrate. It is the only citrus I trust to taste of anything other than sugar.

This salad is less a recipe than a set of small decisions. The fennel gets shaved until a lamp shows through it. The oranges get peeled with a knife, not a peeler, so the white pith goes with the skin and the segments sit clean. The onion soaks in cold water to lose its bite. Then everything meets a dressing so simple it embarrasses me slightly: oil, sherry vinegar and salt, with coriander seed crushed in a mortar until it smells like a bakery.

Eat it at the table, not in the kitchen. The point is the slow arrival of each slice, and the way the olive and the orange argue for a second before agreeing.

Fennel wants to be cut thin enough to see light through it, and oranges want to be sliced without apology.

Mara Okafor, on the first time she made this for a crowd

Ingredients

Serves 4 · 20 minutes, most of it waiting

  • Blood oranges, peeled and cut into rounds3
  • Fennel bulbs, shaved thin, fronds reserved2
  • Small red onion, sliced into half moons1
  • Castelvetrano olives, pitted and torn60 g
  • Extra-virgin olive oil, grassy and green3 tbsp
  • Sherry vinegar1 tbsp
  • Coriander seed, toasted and crushed1 tsp
  • Ricotta salata, shaved80 g
  • Flaky salt and black pepperto taste

Method

  1. 1

    Toast the coriander seed in a dry pan for two minutes, until it ticks and smells warm. Crush it coarsely in a mortar and set aside.

  2. 2

    Peel the oranges with a small knife, taking off the pith with the skin. Slice across into rounds about 5 mm thick and lay them on a wide plate.

  3. 3

    Shave the fennel on a mandoline with the guard on. Keep the fronds whole for later.

  4. 4

    Drop the onion into a bowl of cold water for ten minutes, then drain and pat dry in a towel.

  5. 5

    Whisk the oil, vinegar, a generous pinch of salt and the crushed coriander in a small jar. Shake hard.

  6. 6

    Scatter the fennel over the oranges, then the onion and olives. Shave the ricotta salata over the top.

  7. 7

    Dress just before serving, finish with fronds, pepper and flaky salt. Eat within ten minutes, while the fennel still snaps.
