Blistered Tomatoes, Burnt Honey

On the one week of the year when tomatoes taste like they should, and how to keep it going.

By Elena Marsh · Illustrated in SVG · 35 min · Serves 4

Every summer there is a window, roughly seven days long, when a tomato stops being a tomato and starts being a memory of one. The skin tightens, the sugar comes up through the flesh, and the whole thing smells faintly of vine. Most of us spend the rest of the year chasing that smell in the supermarket and coming back with something mealy and pale.

This recipe is an argument for not overthinking the window. Blister the fruit in a hot pan until the skins split and char in places. Then pour over honey that has been cooked until it turns the colour of an old church window and tastes faintly of toast. The contrast does most of the work: sharp acid, deep caramel, and a salt that wakes everything up.

It is also a dish that asks very little of you. You will need one pan, one bowl, and the patience to leave the fruit alone long enough for it to catch. That last part is harder than it sounds, and it is the whole secret.

“Leave the tomatoes alone. The char is not a mistake; it is the flavour arriving.”

Elena Marsh, on pan discipline

Ingredients

Serves four as a starter, or two as a light lunch with bread.

  • Ripe tomatoes, mixed sizes900 g
  • Runny honey, preferably wildflower3 tbsp
  • Olive oil, good and fruity2 tbsp
  • Sherry vinegar1 tbsp
  • Flaky sea salt1 tsp
  • Fresh basil leaves, torna handful
  • Stale sourdough, to serve4 slices

Method

  1. 1

    Halve the larger tomatoes and leave the small ones whole. Season the cut faces with a pinch of salt and let them sit on a tea towel for ten minutes to drain.

  2. 2

    Heat a heavy pan over high heat until it is nearly smoking. Add the oil, then the tomatoes, cut side down, in a single layer. Do not move them.

  3. 3

    Cook for four to five minutes, until the skins split and the bottoms are deeply blistered. Turn once, briefly, then move everything to a warm plate.

  4. 4

    Lower the heat to medium. Add the honey and let it bubble for about a minute, until it darkens to amber and smells of toast. Splash in the vinegar and take the pan off the heat.

  5. 5

    Toast the bread in the same pan, in the honey and tomato juices. Lay the slices on plates, pile the tomatoes on top, spoon over the pan sauce, and finish with basil and sea salt.
