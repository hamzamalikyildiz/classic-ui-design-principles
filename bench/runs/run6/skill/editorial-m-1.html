Weeknight, Slowly

Brown-butter chicken, sage and a lemon that means it

Butter left in a hot pan until it turns the colour of toasted hazelnut is the whole trick. Everything else just keeps it company.

By Imogen Teague · Serves four · 45 minutes

My grandmother never wrote a recipe down. She made this one on Thursdays when the tin of butter was nearly done, and she would stand at the stove talking about the price of eggs while the pan did the real work. It took me years to notice that the talk was the timer.

Brown butter is butter with its water cooked off and its milk solids allowed to toast. It is nutty, faintly sweet and a little bitter at the edges, which is exactly what chicken thighs want from a pan. Sage goes in with it, crisping at the rims until it tastes like something you would pay for at a good trattoria.

“Watch the butter, not the clock. The clock has never once smelled anything.”

Imogen Teague, from the family notebook

The method

  1. 1

    Pat eight bone-in thighs dry, salt them well on both sides, and leave them uncovered on a rack for twenty minutes while the pan heats.

  2. 2

    Lay the thighs skin side down in a cold cast iron skillet, set it over medium heat, and let the fat render for about fifteen minutes until the skin is deep gold.

  3. 3

    Turn the chicken, lower the heat a notch, and cook four minutes more. Move it to a warm plate and pour off all but two tablespoons of fat.

  4. 4

    Add 80 g of butter and twelve sage leaves. Swirl until the butter foams, then goes quiet and smells of hazelnuts, about three minutes.

  5. 5

    Return the chicken to the pan, spoon the butter over, and finish with the juice of half a lemon and a pinch of flaky salt. Rest five minutes before serving.

Ingredients

Serves four

  • Bone-in chicken thighs8
  • Salted butter80 g
  • Fresh sage leaves12
  • Lemon1/2
  • Flaky sea saltto taste
  • Cast iron skillet30 cm
