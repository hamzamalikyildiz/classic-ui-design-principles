Weeknight, but slower

Charred leek and brown butter orzo

A pot of pasta that tastes like it took an afternoon.

Serves four. Takes forty minutes, most of it hands-off.

Notes from the stove

Leeks are the most patient vegetable in the shop. They sit in the crisper for a week looking unremarkable, and then one evening you slice them lengthwise, lay them cut side down in a hot pan, and they turn sweet and smoky in a way an onion never quite manages.

This dish grew out of a cold October when the only thing left in the fridge was a half bag of orzo, a stick of butter and a bundle of leeks that had seen better days. Browning the butter until it smells of toasted hazelnuts, then loosening the charred leeks with a splash of stock, turned that small pantry into something I now cook for guests.

Do not rush the leeks. The char is the flavour. Let them sit still long enough to colour before you stir, and keep the heat high enough that the pan sizzles when they go in.

Ingredients

For four generous bowls

  • Leeks, white and pale green3
  • Orzo300 g
  • Unsalted butter90 g
  • Olive oil2 tbsp
  • Garlic, smashed2 cloves
  • Vegetable stock, hot750 ml
  • Lemon, zest and juice1
  • Parmesan, finely grated60 g
  • Fresh thyme6 sprigs
  • Flaky salt and black pepperto taste

Method

  1. 1

    Trim the leeks, split them lengthwise and rinse well between the layers. Cut into 5 cm pieces and keep the cut sides facing up.

  2. 2

    Heat the oil in a wide pan over high heat. Lay the leeks cut side down, in a single layer if you can, and leave them alone for three minutes until deeply charred.

  3. 3

    Turn the leeks, add the garlic and thyme, and cook two minutes more. Tip everything onto a plate and set aside.

  4. 4

    In the same pan, melt the butter over medium heat. Swirl it constantly until the foam subsides and the milk solids turn hazelnut brown, about four minutes.

  5. 5

    Add the orzo and toast it in the butter for two minutes, stirring, until the edges look translucent. Pour in the hot stock, return the leeks, and simmer hard.

  6. 6

    Stir every minute or so for 10 to 12 minutes, until the orzo is tender and the sauce is glossy and loose. Add more stock if it tightens too quickly.

  7. 7

    Off the heat, fold in the parmesan, lemon zest and a squeeze of juice. Season generously, then serve straight away with more pepper on top.

“The char is the flavour. Let the leeks sit still long enough to colour before you stir.”
