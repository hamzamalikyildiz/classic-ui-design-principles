Charred Leeks with Brown Butter & Hazelnuts

Why patience at the edge of a cast-iron skillet turns the humblest winter allium into something sweet, smoky, and quietly celebratory.

By Elena Vance • Serves 4 as a side • 35 minutes

For years I treated leeks as background singers—chopped fine, sweated in olive oil, and immediately buried under stock or potatoes. It took a cold November evening in Lyon, watching a line cook lay whole halved leeks cut-side down onto a dry, smoking pan, to realize I had been rushing past their best quality.

When a leek meets high heat without crowding, its outer layers blister into papery charcoal while the pale core steams inside its own wrapper. Once you peel back the blackened jacket, what remains is tender, jammy, and sweet enough to stand beside nutty brown butter and toasted hazelnuts.

“Don’t rescue the pan too early; the char on the outside is what protects the sweetness within.”

Ingredients

  • • 4 medium leeks, trimmed and halved lengthwise
  • • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • • 4 tbsp unsalted butter
  • • 1/3 cup skinned hazelnuts, roughly chopped
  • • 1 tbsp sherry vinegar
  • • Flaky sea salt & black pepper
  • • 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley

Method

  1. 1

    Rinse and dry: Hold the halved leeks under cold running water, fanning the layers gently to wash out any grit, then pat completely dry with a clean towel.

  2. 2

    Sear cut-side down: Heat the olive oil in a large cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat. Lay the leeks cut-side down and cook undisturbed for 6–8 minutes until deeply browned.

  3. 3

    Roast until tender: Flip the leeks, transfer the skillet to a 400°F (200°C) oven, and roast for 12–15 minutes until a knife slides easily into the thickest base.

  4. 4

    Brown the butter: In a small saucepan over medium heat, melt the butter until it foams and smells toasted, about 3 minutes. Stir in the hazelnuts and sherry vinegar off the heat.

  5. 5

    Serve warm: Arrange the leeks on a platter, spoon the warm hazelnut brown butter over top, and finish with flaky salt, black pepper, and parsley.
