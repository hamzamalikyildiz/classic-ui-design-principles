Charred Figs with Brown Butter & Salted Labneh

When late-August figs turn jammy at the stem, a hot cast-iron skillet and nutty brown butter coax out their deepest honeyed notes.

There is a brief fortnight at the end of summer when figs stop behaving like fruit and start behaving like pastry. Their skins thin to the texture of worn suede, and a single drop of amber syrup beads at the base. Eaten raw, they are quiet and floral; touched by high heat, they turn into something altogether darker.

The trick is restraint. Instead of burying them in sugar, sear the cut faces in foaming brown butter until the natural fructose blisters. Against cold, tangy labneh and flaky sea salt, the contrast does all the work.

“Good late-summer cooking is mostly knowing when to take the pan off the flame.”

Method

  1. Spread the labneh: Spoon the chilled labneh onto a wide, shallow platter and swirl it into a thick bed with the back of a spoon. Season lightly with flaky sea salt.
  2. Brown the butter: Melt the unsalted butter in a heavy cast-iron skillet over medium heat. Swirl for 2 to 3 minutes until the milk solids turn hazelnut brown and smell toasted.
  3. Sear the figs: Place the halved figs cut-side down in the foaming butter. Cook undisturbed for 3 minutes until caramelized at the edges, then add rosemary and honey.
  4. Finish & serve: Transfer the warm figs onto the labneh, spooning the warm brown butter and crisp rosemary needles over the top. Serve immediately with torn sourdough.

Ingredients

  • 8 ripe black mission figs, halved
  • 40g unsalted butter
  • 250g thick labneh, chilled
  • 1 tbsp wildflower honey
  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • Flaky sea salt & cracked pepper
