## Run setup and caveats

- Protocol v2 (`bench/PROTOCOL.md`, skill commit `577491a`): 5 moods x 3 brief types (`v` vague, `r` detailed, `m` second domain) x 1 repetition = 15 pairs, 30 pages, 75 blind votes.
- Cross-tier models: pages generated by **Gemini 4 Hydrogen (Low)** (weak/fast generator; both arms had up to 2 fix rounds with rendered PNGs), judged blindly by 5 **Gemini 4 Neon (Medium)** persona judges (`judges.json`).
- **A/B balance:** `make_judge_sets.py` enforced an exact per-judge 50/50 split (`7` or `8` of `15` `skill=A`), yielding `39/75` (`52%`) raw `A` selections overall (no significant position bias).
- **Length comparison (after removing rigid mood Content floors):** mean lines `251.5` (`skill`) vs `204.4` (`noskill`), mean size `14.9 KB` vs `11.3 KB`. Notably `technical` (`201.3 vs 260.3 lines`, `13.4 vs 13.4 KB`) was actually shorter/lighter in `skill` than `noskill`, while `trust` (`334.7 vs 228.3 lines`) and `bold` (`257.0 vs 175.7 lines`) kept richer structure.
- **Honest interpretation:** With a weaker generator (`Hydrogen Low`) and strict `Neon` judges on a 15-pair sample (`CI 49%–87%`, `p = 0.3018`), `bold` (`3/3`), `playful` (`3/3`), `trust` (`3/3`), and vague briefs (`v`: `4/5 = 84%` votes) showed clear wins and automatic quality stayed clean (`axe serious 0/15` vs `11/15`, `--check WARNs 0.0` vs `0.9`). However, `technical` (`0/3`) and `editorial` (`1/3`) lost when the relaxed floor led `Hydrogen Low` to produce sparse panels or miss a full editorial figure/structure that `noskill` accidentally included. Judges are still role-playing LLMs rather than human raters.
