Weeknight Italian, slowed down

The sauce that taught me to wait

Forty minutes of heat cannot fix a tomato. Two and a half hours of quiet can, and the result is a sugo that tastes like it was planned for a month.

By Marta Reyes

Tested three times · 2 hr 40 min · Serves 6

Why slow is the whole trick

Most tomato sauces fail in the first ten minutes. The cook adds everything at once, turns the heat up to hurry things along, and ends with something bright and thin that tastes mostly of onion. The fix is not a secret ingredient. It is patience with the pot, and a willingness to let the water leave the fruit before anything else happens.

My grandmother never measured anything. She kept a wooden spoon in the pot and returned to it every few minutes, scraping the bottom and tipping the lid to see what the steam was doing. This recipe is my attempt to write down those returns so that a cook who is not standing in her kitchen can still arrive at the same place.

Tomatoes do not need to be rushed. They need to be left alone long enough to forget what they were.

Marta Reyes, from her notebook

What you will need

Use the ripest plum tomatoes you can find. Pale, watery fruit will cook down into something flat, however long you wait.

  • Ripe plum tomatoes1.2 kg
  • Extra virgin olive oil3 tbsp
  • Yellow onion, finely chopped1
  • Garlic cloves, smashed4
  • Fennel seed, lightly toasted1 tsp
  • Chili flakes1/2 tsp
  • Tomato paste2 tbsp
  • Dry white wine200 ml
  • Parmesan rind60 g
  • Sugar, fine salt, basilto taste

The method

  1. 1

    Warm the oil over medium-low heat, add the onion with a pinch of salt, and cook for twelve minutes until it is soft and sweet, not coloured.

  2. 2

    Add the garlic, fennel and chili and stir for two minutes. Stir in the tomato paste and let it darken slightly on the bottom of the pan.

  3. 3

    Pour in the wine and let it bubble until nearly gone. Add the tomatoes, crushed by hand, along with the cheese rind and a teaspoon of sugar.

  4. 4

    Cover with the lid set slightly open and simmer very gently for ninety minutes. Scrape the bottom every fifteen minutes and do not let it boil hard.

  5. 5

    Remove the lid and cook for another thirty minutes, until the sauce coats the back of a spoon and the oil rises at the edges.

  6. 6

    Fish out the rind, season with salt, tear in the basil and rest for ten minutes. Serve over pasta with more oil and a grind of pepper.

Equipment

  • A heavy 4 litre pot with a tight lid
  • A wooden spoon with a flat edge
  • A box grater or a sieve for the tomatoes
  • A ladle for the final stir

Make ahead

  1. Day beforeCrush the tomatoes, toast the fennel and chill both overnight.
  2. Two hours beforeSoften the onion and build the base in the pot.
  3. Serving dayReheat gently with a splash of water and finish with basil.

Yield

About a quarter of the weight leaves as steam; the rest becomes sauce.

Wine on the table

The same dry white that goes into the pot works well in the glass. Something light and mineral, served cool.
