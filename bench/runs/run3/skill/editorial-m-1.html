Charred Tomato Panzanella, Made Slowly

A bread salad is only as good as the weeks you spent waiting for the tomatoes. Here is how to stretch the wait into dinner.

By Marisol Vance, kitchen notes from a farm stand in the Hudson Valley

Every August my neighbour sends a crate of misshapen tomatoes over the fence with a note that says only "too many." That crate is the reason this salad exists. The fruit is split, sun-blistered and sweeter than anything you can buy in a supermarket, and it wants to be eaten the same day.

The trick is heat. A hot cast-iron pan turns the cut faces dark and sticky in about four minutes, and that char carries the sugar, smoke and a little bitterness that cuts through the bread. Do not rush it. The pan should hiss, not sizzle.

Stale bread is not a failure here; it is the point. Torn sourdough soaks up the tomato juice and vinegar and turns soft in the middle while the crust stays firm enough to bite. Give it fifteen minutes, then toss again just before serving so nothing goes limp.

"The bread should drink the tomato, not drown in it."

Marisol Vance, on the second pan

What you need

Serves four as a main, or six as a starter.

  • Ripe heirloom tomatoes, mixed900 g
  • Sourdough, a day old, torn250 g
  • Red wine vinegar3 tbsp
  • Extra-virgin olive oil, peppery90 ml
  • Red onion, thinly sliced1 small
  • Capers, drained2 tbsp
  • Basil leaves and flaky salt1 handful

Method

  1. 1

    Heat a cast-iron pan until a drop of water skitters. Halve the largest tomatoes and set them cut-side down. Leave them alone for four minutes.

  2. 2

    Turn the tomatoes and char the skins for one minute more. Tip the lot into a bowl with every juice from the board.

  3. 3

    Fry the torn bread in the same pan with two tablespoons of oil until the edges are gold, about three minutes. Toss it straight into the tomato juice.

  4. 4

    Whisk the vinegar with the remaining oil and a pinch of salt. Pour over, add the onion and capers, and let it stand for fifteen minutes.

  5. 5

    Fold in the basil with your hands, not a spoon, and finish with flaky salt. Serve at once, while the bread still has some chew.

No heirlooms? Substitute

Cherry tomatoes
Halve and char the same way; they cook faster, so watch the pan.
Sherry vinegar
Softer than red wine vinegar and a good stand-in if the salad tastes sharp.
Stale baguette
Use the heel end; the crumb holds its shape better than a loaf.
