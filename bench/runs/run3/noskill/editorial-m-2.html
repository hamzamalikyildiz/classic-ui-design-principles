Brown Butter Gnocchi, Made Slowly

Pillowy potato dumplings, a pan of butter cooked until it smells like toasted hazelnuts, and a handful of sage that crisps at the edges. Simple, and worth the wait.

By Marisol Vance · Serves 4 · 1 hour 15 minutes

Gnocchi has a reputation for being fussy, and it earns it on the days the potatoes are waterlogged or the flour is added in a rush. The trick is almost entirely about moisture. Bake the potatoes whole rather than boiling them, let the steam escape, and push the flesh through a sieve while it is still warm enough to breathe.

My grandmother made hers on a floured board with a fork, rolling each piece against the tines until it curled. I still do that on Sundays. On Tuesdays I use a bench scraper and accept the ridges are less neat. Both work. What matters is that the dough is soft, not sticky, and that you stop kneading the moment it holds together. Overworked gnocchi turns to rubber, and no amount of butter will save it.

Ingredients

For the gnocchi and the butter

  • 1 kg starchy potatoes, such as Russet or Maris Piper
  • 1 large egg yolk
  • 150 g plain flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1 tsp fine salt, plus extra for the water
  • 120 g unsalted butter
  • 12 fresh sage leaves
  • 40 g Parmesan, finely grated
  • Black pepper, freshly cracked

Method

  1. 1

    Heat the oven to 200°C. Prick the potatoes all over and bake them directly on the rack for 55 to 65 minutes, until a knife slides in with no resistance.

  2. 2

    Halve the potatoes while hot and scoop out the flesh. Press it through a fine sieve onto a clean tray and spread it out to cool for ten minutes.

  3. 3

    Make a well in the potato. Add the yolk, salt and most of the flour. Bring it together gently with a bench scraper, adding flour only until the dough stops sticking to your hands.

  4. 4

    Divide into four pieces. Roll each into a rope about the width of your thumb, then cut into 2 cm pieces. Roll each over a fork or ridged board if you like the grooves.

  5. 5

    Bring a wide pan of salted water to a gentle simmer. Cook the gnocchi in batches for 2 to 3 minutes, until they float. Lift them out with a slotted spoon.

  6. 6

    Melt the butter in a wide skillet over medium heat. Add the sage and let the butter foam, then turn golden and nutty, about 4 minutes. Toss the gnocchi in it for 30 seconds and finish with Parmesan and pepper.
