Brown Butter White Bean Toast

Nutty, bright and ready before the kettle boils.

By Rosa Acheson•Serves 2•20 minutes

Some dinners need a plan. This one needs a pan and a little patience. For years I treated beans on toast as the lazy cousin of a real meal, until a cook I worked beside made butter go the colour of hazelnuts and tip a spoonful of it over a plain can of cannellini. It was a revelation: the sweetness of the milk solids, the bitter edge, the way fat carries a whole room of flavour.

The trick is restraint. Brown the butter slowly and watch it like a hawk, because the difference between fragrant and scorched is about thirty seconds. Smash the beans roughly so some stay whole and some turn creamy, and finish with lemon, because the richness wants something to cut through it.

“The best dishes are often the ones that ask you to slow down for a single minute.”

Rosa Acheson

Ingredients

For two generous slices.

  • Sourdough or thick country bread2 slices
  • Unsalted butter50 g
  • Cannellini beans, drained1 can (400 g)
  • Garlic clove, finely grated1
  • Lemon, zest and juice1
  • Fresh sage leaves8
  • Chilli flakesa pinch
  • Flaky salt and black pepperto taste

Method

  1. 1

    Toast the bread until deeply golden and rub each slice with the cut side of the garlic clove while it is still warm.

  2. 2

    Melt the butter in a small heavy pan over medium heat. Swirl often as it foams, then settles, then turns hazelnut brown with a nutty smell. Pull it off the heat straight away.

  3. 3

    Warm the beans in a second pan with a splash of their liquid. Mash about half against the side of the pan so the texture is part creamy, part whole.

  4. 4

    Fry the sage leaves in a teaspoon of the brown butter for about 30 seconds until crisp. Lift them out onto kitchen paper.

  5. 5

    Spoon the beans over the toast, add the lemon juice and a little zest, then pour over the remaining brown butter. Scatter the sage, chilli flakes and a grind of pepper, and serve at once.
