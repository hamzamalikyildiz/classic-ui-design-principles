Recipe · Late summer

Blistered tomatoes on toast, and the patience they ask for

Hot skillet, a handful of sun-warm tomatoes and a crust of bread that has to earn its butter. Nothing here is hard. It only asks that you stand still and wait for the pan.

By Ines Carvalho · Serves 4 · 35 minutes

Essay

Every summer my grandmother had the same argument with her stove. She wanted the skillet hotter than it would go, and the stove wanted to be left alone. The tomatoes settled it. Cut in half and set cut side down, they hiss, catch, and slowly give up their sugar in a dark, uneven char that tastes like a longer afternoon than it was.

Toast is the vehicle, not the point. Use a loaf with a chewy middle and a crust thick enough to hold oil. Rub the warm slice with a cut garlic clove until it turns to paste, then let the tomato juice soak into the holes. What you end up with is a plate that tastes of heat and salt, with nothing hiding behind it.

“The pan is never ready when you think it is. Wait a minute longer, then wait another.”
Ines Carvalho, on her grandmother's skillet

Serve it the moment the bread leaves the pan. The butter should still be foaming, the tomatoes should still be sliding, and the first bite should be too hot to talk through.

Ingredients

For four slices of toast

  • Ripe tomatoes500 g
  • Sourdough, thick cut4 slices
  • Olive oil4 tbsp
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Flaky salt1 tsp
  • Black pepper, crackedto taste
  • Basil leavesa small handful
  • Sherry vinegar1 tsp

Method

  1. 1

    Heat a heavy skillet over medium-high for four minutes. It is ready when a drop of water skips across the surface and vanishes.

  2. 2

    Halve the tomatoes across the equator and lay them cut side down in the dry pan. Leave them alone for three minutes while the skins blister and the edges darken.

  3. 3

    Turn the tomatoes once, press gently with a spatula, and cook two minutes more until they collapse into a loose, jammy pulp. Add a pinch of salt and remove from heat.

  4. 4

    Brush the bread with two tablespoons of oil and toast it in the same pan, pressing down for the first minute, until golden at the edges and deeply browned in the middle.

  5. 5

    Rub each warm slice with the cut garlic. Spoon the tomatoes over, add the vinegar, a drizzle of oil, pepper and basil, and eat before the bread softens.
