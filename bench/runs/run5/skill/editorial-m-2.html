Weeknight · Late autumn

Charred leeks, brown butter, and the patience to wait for them

Most leek recipes ask you to soften them. This one asks you to scorch them a little, then get out of the way.

By Mara Hollis · 12 min read · Serves 4 · Ready in 35 minutes

Introduction

My grandmother never let a leek near a flame. Leeks were for soup, she said, and soup was for sick people and Sundays. It took me years to understand that she was right about the soup and wrong about everything else. A leek that has been given time against hot iron turns sweet in a way that no amount of gentle simmering will reach, and the char that gathers on its layers tastes faintly of toast and smoke.

The trick is restraint. Do not stir. Do not rush to turn them. Let the cut faces darken until they look close to ruined, because that is the point where the sugars change. Then butter goes into the same pan, cooks until it smells of hazelnuts and turns the color of weak tea, and a splash of sharp sherry vinegar pulls the whole thing into focus.

This is a dish for a Tuesday with one good pan and an open window. It needs almost nothing else, and it is better for being served on a plain plate with bread torn by hand.

— Mara Hollis, from her notebook, October

Method

  1. 1

    Halve four large leeks lengthwise, leaving the root end intact. Rinse between the layers, then dry them well with a towel. Wet leeks steam instead of charring.

  2. 2

    Heat a heavy cast iron pan over medium-high for five minutes. Add the leeks cut side down with no oil. Leave them alone for twelve minutes, until the cut faces are dark gold and blistered.

  3. 3

    Turn the leeks once and cook for four minutes more, until the layers feel soft when pierced with a knife. Move them to a warm plate.

  4. 4

    Lower the heat to medium. Add 60 g butter and swirl until it foams, then settles and browns, about three minutes. Watch for the smell of hazelnut and pull the pan off the heat.

  5. 5

    Add one tablespoon of sherry vinegar to the butter. It will spit, so stand back. Spoon the sauce over the leeks, finish with flaky salt and a grind of black pepper, and serve while the edges still crackle.

Ingredients

For four, as a side or a light supper

  • Large leeks4
  • Unsalted butter60 g
  • Sherry vinegar1 tbsp
  • Flaky sea saltto taste
  • Black pepper, crackeda few turns
  • Day-old sourdoughto serve

Use a pan you trust. Thin pans hot-spot and burn the butter before the leeks have had their turn.
