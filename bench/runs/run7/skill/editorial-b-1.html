Night Train to Trieste

There is a point, somewhere after the last lights of the mainland, when the carriage stops feeling like a room and starts feeling like a decision you already made.

By Ilse Marrow · 9 min read · Issue 14, Autumn

The 21:48 leaves Venice without ceremony. No announcement, no crowd at the barrier, only a conductor who checks the couchette list twice and then tells you to lock the door. I had planned to read on the way. Instead I watched the canal lamps slide off the window and listened to the wheels find their rhythm, a double knock every few seconds that sounds like someone patiently tapping a table.

Trieste is not on the way to anything, and that is most of its charm. The city sits with its back to Slovenia and its face to a gulf that has belonged to several empires in turn, so its streets carry three names for many squares. The night train is the only approach that feels like it respects this. You arrive while the country is still deciding whether to wake up.

The timetable is a small argument between old and new. The line still runs the way it did when the empire needed its ports connected, through flat fields of maize and poplar, past stations with more paint than passengers. Each stop is a single lit platform and a bench. At Cervignano, just after midnight, one man smoked under a sign and nodded at me as if I had been expected.

I slept badly, which is the honest way to take a night train. Each time I surfaced the window had changed: first the wide dark of the plain, then a faint smear of hills, then a sense that the air had grown colder and saltier before I could see any water at all. You smell the Adriatic before the train admits it is close.

Overnight, Venezia to Trieste

  1. 21:48Venezia Santa Lucia
    Board early. Couchettes sit in the rear carriages.
  2. 22:31Mestre
    The last of the mainland lights.
  3. 23:12Portogruaro
    One lamp, one bench, one bell.
  4. 00:04Cervignano
    Night stop. Stay in your berth.
  5. 04:38Monfalcone
    Shipyard cranes and the first sea air.
  6. 05:14Trieste Centrale
    Coffee on the platform, then walk to the water.

Berth: couchette, six to a compartment.

Tickets: book the sleeper ahead; the supplement is the price of sleep.

Luggage: keep one bag under the bunk.

“A night train does not ask where you are going. It asks only whether you can stay awake long enough to notice the change.”

From the journey, Cervignano

By the time the carriage lights came up at five, I had stopped counting stations. The last stretch runs along the coast in grey half-light, past sheds, salt flats and the long concrete flank of the shipyard at Monfalcone. Then the track bends, the sea opens out on the left, and the platform at Trieste Centrale is full of people carrying coffee as if nothing unusual had happened at all.

That is what I would tell anyone planning this trip. Book the couchette, not the seat. Pay for the sleep, because the sleep is the part you will remember. Arrive with nothing to do until the cafés open on the Piazza Unità, which they will, slowly, while the sun comes up over the water and the city remembers its own name.
