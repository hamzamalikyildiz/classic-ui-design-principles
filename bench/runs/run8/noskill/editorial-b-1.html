Thermodynamics & Craft

The Roasting Curve

Twelve minutes inside a cast-iron drum separates grassy green seed from caramelized sweetness. Understanding how heat, airflow, and rate of rise shape every cup.

Green coffee arrives at the roastery dense, pale, and smelling faintly of cut hay. Inside each seed sits roughly eleven percent moisture bound within a tight cellulose matrix. When the roaster drops a twelve-kilogram charge into a preheated drum, the bean temperature plummets as cold seeds absorb ambient heat, reaching a turning point around ninety seconds in.

From that trough, the craft is a balancing act of momentum. During the drying phase, water must leave the bean evenly before surface sugars begin to brown. Rush this stage with too much gas, and the exterior scorches while the center stays raw and astringent.

“First crack is not the finish line; it is the moment the bean finally tells you how much energy it has left.”

— Roastery Floor Notes, Portland

Past 150°C, the Maillard reaction accelerates: amino acids and reducing sugars recombine into hundreds of aromatic compounds, shifting the seed from pale yellow to warm cinnamon. Around 196°C, trapped steam ruptures the cell walls in an audible chorus known as first crack. From there, every ten seconds of development time trades bright, floral acidity for deeper body and chocolate sweetness.

Telemetry Log

Roast Stages

  1. 1. Drying Phase 0:00 – 4:15

    95°C → 150°C

    Moisture evaporates; beans shift from emerald green to pale hay.

  2. 2. Maillard Browning 4:15 – 8:45

    150°C → 195°C

    Sugars and amino acids caramelize; toasted-bread aroma emerges.

  3. 3. First Crack 8:45 – 9:40

    196°C → 200°C

    Exothermic steam release fractures bean structure; chaff sheds.

  4. 4. Development & Drop 9:40 – 11:30

    200°C → 206°C

    Airflow rises, gas tapers; beans cool rapidly on the perforated tray.
