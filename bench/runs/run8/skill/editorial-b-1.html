The Roasting Curve

How twelve minutes of controlled heat transform dense, grassy green coffee seeds into balanced, aromatic beans.

By Tomasz Kowalski · October 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Inside a cast-iron drum roaster, a batch of washed Ethiopian coffee undergoes nearly two thousand chemical reactions in less than twelve minutes. To the uninitiated, roasting looks like simple baking. In practice, it is a race against momentum: heat must be applied aggressively enough to drive moisture out of the seed’s cellular core, yet tapered early enough that the sugars do not carbonize before the center catches up.

Drying and the Turning Point

Green coffee arrives at the roaster holding roughly eleven percent moisture. When cold beans drop into a drum preheated to 195°C, the thermocouple plummets until the beans and metal reach thermal equilibrium—the turning point. For the next four minutes, energy goes almost entirely into evaporating free water. Rush this phase, and the exterior scorches while the core stays raw and astringent.

Maillard Browning and First Crack

Around 150°C, the beans shift from pale hay to toasted cinnamon. Amino acids and reducing sugars react in the Maillard cascade, building the nutty, chocolate, and stone-fruit aromatics that define a cup. Minutes later, internal steam pressure ruptures the bean’s cellulose wall with an audible snap like popcorn: first crack.

“First crack is not the finish line; it is the moment the roaster’s real judgment begins. Fifteen seconds too long, and delicate jasmine turns to ash.”

In the final development phase, the roaster cuts gas pressure so the rate of rise tapers smoothly. Dropping the batch onto the cooling tray at 206°C locks in crisp acidity alongside caramelized sweetness.

Roast-Stage Reference

1. Charge & Turning Point
0:00 – 1:30 · 195°C → 82°C
Cold beans absorb drum heat until thermal equilibrium.
2. Drying Phase
1:30 – 4:45 · 82°C → 150°C
Moisture evaporates; beans turn from green to pale yellow.
3. Maillard Reaction
4:45 – 9:15 · 150°C → 196°C
Browning reactions build body and aromatic complexity.
4. First Crack
9:15 – 10:15 · 196°C → 201°C
Exothermic steam release fractures the bean’s cell wall.
5. Development & Drop
10:15 – 11:30 · 201°C → 206°C
Final 18% of roast time balances acidity and sweetness.
